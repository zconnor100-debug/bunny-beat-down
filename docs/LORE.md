# The Lore of Bunny Beat Down

The world's story, as the Warren tells it, and where it's going. The in-game
text lives in `src/bosses/boss_lore.gd` (BossLore). This page holds the same
story, plus the parts that aren't in the game yet: the quest line it's
building toward.

## The world

- **The Warren** is everyone: the bunnies in their acorn-cup helmets, the
  squirrels, foxes, raccoons, frogs, beavers and hamsters, and the 49 rogues
  of the cast. They live on **Wildwood Island** and settle their differences
  the only way they know: a beat-down, last critter standing.
- **The Burrow** is home: acorns to spend, the Workshop and the Coat Rack. The
  **Trophy Room** keeps your record, and the **Rogues' Gallery** the cast's.
- **The Warren Gazette** reports what the rogues get up to while you're away,
  and holds the front page for a special edition when a giant falls or the
  Barn holds, with a photo of the moment.
- **The Briar**, the thorny wall that closes in on every match, is the
  Starfall Acorn's roots, still looking for its pieces. (The Kernel Hunt's
  Finale, *The Starfall*, below.)
- **The House on the shore**, across the lake from the island, is where the
  Warren goes when the people are out: the children's toy room (**Toy Box
  Bash**) and the hamster cage (**the Hamster Cage**).

## The Night the Acorn Fell

> Ask anyone in the Warren how the giants came and they'll tell it the same
> way, usually with a torch under their chin. One midsummer night a star fell
> over Wildwood. It wasn't a star. It was an acorn, gold and humming, as big as
> the Big Red Barn, and it hit Boulder Bluff so hard the lake jumped. It split
> three ways. The biggest piece rolled down into the Carrot Patch. The second
> skipped across the lake and in through an open window of the House on the
> shore, into a toy box. The last, no bigger than a seed, fell plink into a
> hamster's water bottle. Whatever ate a piece, or was wound by one, or drank
> one, grew. And grew. And went wrong. The Warren calls the pieces the
> Starfall Kernels. Nobody has ever held one: the giants carry them inside.

The Starfall Acorn's glow is gold-green. It's the colour of every reward the
bosses' ladders give (the *Starfall* tracers, muzzle flash and reticle).

## The giants

Each boss's story is told in four chapters on the Trophy Room's **Bosses**
page. The first is the legend everyone knows. The second opens once you've
faced the boss in a Boss Battle, the third once you've beaten it, and the last
once you've beaten it three times. Each last chapter points across the lake, at
the other two.

### Brutus, the Meadow's Nightmare (Wildwood Island)

*DAYS SINCE BRUTUS: 0*

1. **Little Bru.** The runt of the Carrot Patch warren, the shy one who always
   shared, who liked lying in the long grass counting stars. He was counting
   them the night one fell.
2. **Nine Days.** He ate the glowing carrots and the Kernel they'd grown
   round, and grew for nine days. The Big Red Barn keeps a tally on its door:
   DAYS SINCE BRUTUS. It has never got past nine.
3. **The Dent.** The Acorn hit him on its way down; the dent in his skull never
   healed. That's his weak spot (head 2x, jaw 2.5x). Jump the shockwave; run
   before the boulder flies.
4. **What He Hears.** He sleeps under Boulder Bluff and wakes when the ground
   drums. Old Mother Clover, who taught him to count, says he's lonely:
   *"He can hear the other two. Humming. Across the lake."*

In the game: the Thumpergeist model (Brian Trepanier, CC BY 4.0), Ground
Pound, Boulder Toss, Leap, Grab and Roar. Beat him for **the Brutus coat**:
matted grey, torn by claw rakes that glow red and beat like a heart.

### The Tin Tyrant, the Wind-Up Warlord (Toy Box Bash)

*"WIND HIM UP AND WATCH HIM GO!" (on the box he came in)*

1. **Model No. 7.** Three steps, a beep and sparks; thirty years on the toy
   room windowsill, wound so often his key wore shiny. Then the window was open.
2. **The Key Turns Itself.** The Kernel lodged in his winding drum. He wound
   himself, grew taller than the bookcase, climbed Block Castle, crowned
   himself with a bottle cap and read out the Clockwork Decree: *every toy shall
   be wound, and every toy that will not be wound shall be UNWOUND.*
3. **Thirty-Some Turns.** Every half a minute or so he has to wind down: the key
   in his back slows and he's soft (the key is 2.8x). Stay out of the red line
   his eyes paint; when his arms go up, the missiles are coming.
4. **Under the Lid.** The children shut him in the toy box. They should have
   locked it. Red light sweeps under the lid at night. The Jack-in-the-Box says
   *"It's looking for the rest of itself."*

In the game: the Creepy Old Robot Toy model (klaimtrev, CC BY 4.0), eye
lasers, missile barrage, claw clamp, wind-down, Overdrive. Beat him for **the
Tin Tyrant gun skin**: riveted tin painted like an old toy, with a red light
sweeping its seams like the one under the lid.

### Nibbles, the Rabid (the Hamster Cage)

*He ran twelve kilometres a night. Now he runs at you.*

1. **Good Boy.** A golden hamster with a squeaky wheel and very full cheeks,
   loved by the children; sunflower seeds were the whole of his life.
2. **Water Bottle Falls.** The seed-sized Kernel fell into his water bottle.
   By lunchtime he'd chewed through the Big Wheel; by teatime he was foaming.
   The bottle has never once run dry.
3. **He Can't Stop.** He charges whatever moves and can't stop for walls:
   stand in front of something hard and let him hit it. Stay out of the foam.
4. **Tunnels.** Under the bedding he's dug tunnels deeper than any cage should
   go, all running toward the toy room. Toward the hum. *The children say he's
   lonely. The children are closer.*

In the game: the Curious Golden Hamster model (restore50, CC BY 4.0), the
charge, bite, foam spit, burrow. Beat him for **the Rabid froth** beat-down
burst.

### Old Hoot, the Night Watch (Wildwood Island, by night)

*Be home before the first hoot.*

Not one of the Kernel's giants: she was only flying past. The island's other
boss, by night (a Boss Battle's pick there), and the first that flies.

1. **The Night Watch.** Every kit in the Warren is told it at bedtime: be home
   before the first hoot. A snowy owl as white as frost, she's kept the night
   watch over Wildwood since before the Warren dug its first burrow, from the
   tallest pine on Boulder Bluff, counting every critter still out after dark.
   The ones still out by the third hoot aren't counted again.
2. **The Sliver.** She was on the wing the night the Starfall Acorn came down,
   and it passed so close it singed her feathers. A sliver of its husk went
   into her left eye and stayed there, glowing gold-green, and she has grown
   with every night she's watched through it: as wide as the Big Red Barn now.
   She hasn't slept since.
3. **Grounding Her.** Silent in the air: watch the ground for the red ring
   she's about to drop into and get out of it, or she'll carry you off and let
   go from high up (jump, and keep jumping, and you'll wriggle loose). Shoot
   her wings as she dives and she comes down hard; floundering in the grass,
   her eye is right there. When she puffs up to hoot, get out from under her.
4. **Looking Up.** Since the Acorn went home she watches the new star over
   the Bluff more than she watches the Warren, and some nights flies up until
   she's a speck against it. Old Mother Clover says the sliver in her eye
   wants to go home too. *She isn't hunting us. She's trying to get up there,
   and she's angry she can't.*

In the game: the Gliding Snowy Owl model (Pigcraft, CC BY 4.0), rigged with
her wings on its arms' bones and her talons on its feet's, the pupil of her
left eye painted gold-green and glowing (`OwlBoss`). She flies on her own,
not along the navmesh: circling whoever she's after up high, banking into her
turns, her body pitched into a dive. The Snatch, the Talon Rake, the Feather
Volley, the Hoot, and a perch now and then; grounded by shots on her wings
mid-dive. Beat her for **the Feathers** beat-down burst.

### Crackjaw, the Nutcracker General (Toy Box Bash)

*Carved to fight the Mouse King. He's decided you'll do.*

Not one of the Kernel's giants either: he bit a chip off the second piece on
its way past. The Toy Box's other boss (a Boss Battle's pick there).

1. **The Mantel Guard.** Two hundred Christmases on the toy room's
   mantelpiece: a nutcracker in a red coat and a tall red hat, carved by a
   toymaker who swore he put a soldier's heart in every one. He was made to
   fight the Mouse King. The Mouse King never came; he cracked the walnuts
   and waited, and the children named him for the noise his jaw makes.
2. **The Cannonball.** The second piece of the Acorn came through the window
   like a cannonball and hit the mantelpiece first. He caught it in his jaws
   the way he'd caught ten thousand walnuts and bit down: a chip of its shell
   broke off in his mouth before it bounced away into the toy box, and he
   swallowed it. By morning he was taller than the bookcase, his painted eyes
   glowed, and every critter he saw was the Mouse King's army. A bunny in an
   acorn helmet is a nut.
3. **Timber!** Wood all the way through (carrots barely scratch the lacquer,
   and the hat is mostly hat). When the drum rolls, get out of the red lane;
   jump his sabre as it sweeps the floor; when he bows with his jaw wide, get
   out from in front, and if he misses, shoot him in the mouth. Best of all,
   shoot his legs: he goes over like a felled pine, and his head's right there.
4. **Waiting for Orders.** Every night he marches the toy room and stops at
   the toy box to salute it, and stands to attention when the red light
   sweeps under its lid: the Jack-in-the-Box says he's waiting for orders.
   And under the bedding in the Hamster Cage something has been digging
   toward the toy room for weeks. He's heard it. *He thinks the Mouse King is
   coming at last.*

In the game: the Cracxus festivus model (Brian Trepanier, CC BY 4.0, who
made Brutus's too), rigged with his arms, legs, jaw and hat pinned rigid to
their bones, a wooden toy's pegs (`NutcrackerBoss`), his painted irises
glowing ice blue (red when he's angry), a cavalry sabre made in code in his
fist. Quick March (a drum roll, then down a red lane, his boots kicking toy
forts apart), the Sabre Sweep (jump it), Crack!, the Walnut Volley, Present
Arms, and Timber! (shoot his legs). Beat him for **the Nutshells** beat-down
burst.

### Noodle, the Burrow Hunter (the Hamster Cage)

*Every kit knows how the rhyme ends.*

Not one of the Kernel's giants either: she ate Nibbles's larder, every seed
of it soaked in water from his bottle. The Hamster Cage's other boss (a Boss
Battle's pick there).

1. **The Old Rhyme.** The Warren's kits have a skipping rhyme about the long,
   slinking things the big folk sent down rabbit holes: round and round the
   burrow, faster and faster, and on the last word everyone jumps. Noodle is
   the House's ferret, sable brown with a black bandit's mask, a thief of
   socks, who has never seen a rabbit and was bred for two thousand years to
   go down a hole after one.
2. **Nibbles's Larder.** She found the tunnels Nibbles dug toward the toy
   room, and at the end of the deepest his larder: cheekful after cheekful of
   sunflower seeds, every one soaked in water from the bottle that never runs
   dry. She stole the lot, stashed it and ate her stash, and by morning she
   was as long as the Big Wheel is wide.
3. **Pop!** She digs in (stay out from behind her) and goes under, and a
   music box plays the rhyme under the bedding as she comes: on POP! she
   bursts up out of the red ring. Up out of her hole she stops to look round:
   bop her on the nose, hard enough, and she sees stars. Her war dance tires
   her out, and she sleeps like the dead. Don't be close when she wakes.
4. **Finders Keepers.** Her stash, at the bottom of Nibbles's deepest tunnel:
   every sock gone from the House this year, a thimble, three keys, the
   remote, seeds enough for a sunflower field, and at the very bottom
   something small and gold-green that hums, that she curls round when she
   sleeps. *Old Mother Clover says the Acorn didn't take all of itself home.
   "Finders keepers. Ask the ferret."*

In the game: the Ferret model (SDPM Esare, CC BY 4.0), rigged with a two-part
tail, her mouth split along its lips so it opens on her fangs and tongue
(`FerretBoss`), her painted eyes glowing violet (hot pink when she's angry,
dark while she's asleep or under the bedding). Long and low: ten critters
tall only reared up on her hind legs. Pop Goes the Weasel (a music box's
rhyme under the bedding; out of the hamster's burrows when one's near;
bopped on the nose while she's up, she's seeing stars), the Bedding Spray
as she digs in, the Weasel War Dance (ending in a dead sleep and a poof of
musk), the Lunge, and the Bottle-Brush. Tube Town rattles as she goes by
under it, and if you hide in its tubes she comes up under them and sends
them flying. She bounds like a ferret, stretched long in the air, and
sleeps coiled up nose to tail. Beat her for **the Finders Keepers**
beat-down burst.

### The Briar Heart, the Acorn's Roots (Boulder Bluff, by night)

*Every match, it closes in. Now you know why.*

Not a giant of the Boss Battles: the Kernel Hunt's last boss. Its chapters
open in the Finale (faced, beaten, beaten three times).

1. **The Roots.** When the Acorn split three ways, what was left of it, the
   husk, sank into Boulder Bluff and put down roots, and they've grown under
   Wildwood ever since, under the lake and the House, looking for the three
   pieces. That's why the Briar closes in wherever critters fight: it thinks
   the noise is them.
2. **The Heart.** Where the husk lies the roots knotted into a heart, a knot
   of thorn as big as the Big Red Barn, and it beats. It keeps the Kernels
   shut in thorny pods on vines of their own and opens them one at a time to
   drink: that's when to shoot.
3. **Starved.** Cut its roots and it starves; break its pods and the Kernels
   fly free. With the last out it splits open, and there's only the light it
   drank, burning, and the Briar closing in one last time.
4. **Number One Hundred and Thirteen.** Old Mother Clover says the Acorn only
   ever wanted to go home, and the Briar only ever wanted to hold on to it.
   There's a new star over the Bluff now. Little Bru will tell you which.

In the game: built in code, no model (a heart of nested loops of thorny vine
in the split husk of the Acorn, VineMesh), rooted, turning to face whoever
it's after. Its pods (shut, they shrug carrots off; open, they don't), the
Thorn Volley, the Root Burst, the Lash round its foot (jump it), and, split
open, its heart. Beat it for **the Shooting stars** beat-down burst.

## Where it's going: the Kernel Hunt (a PvE quest line)

The three last chapters set it up: the Kernels hum for each other. Brutus
listens across the lake, the Tin Tyrant sweeps the dark under its lid, and
Nibbles digs toward the toy room. The oldest stories say the Briar is the
Acorn's roots, still growing and still looking for its pieces, which is why it
closes in wherever critters fight. Nobody knows what happens when the pieces
find each other.

**The Kernel Hunt** is the player-versus-environment mode: you and a squad of
rogues against the **Touched**, critters the Acorn's light has got into (any
species, gold-green light running through their fur in veins, silent, run by
the bots' brain as an enemy team), through acts of missions that end in the
boss fights already in the game. Its page is on the main menu. The prologue
and Acts I, II and III and the Finale are playable.

### The prologue: *Days Since Brutus* (playable)

The tally on the Big Red Barn's door says nine days since anyone saw Brutus.
It has never got past nine. You start at the Barn with the **Barn Rangers**,
the Warren's volunteer watch: Buck Toothington (who keeps the tally), Dam Dan,
Hazel, Kernel, Duchess, Captain Carrot and Lily Pad Lou, each at a post round
the Barn. The Touched come in three waves, out of the Carrot Patch and then
round the sides, the last with a Touched giant (a beaver the size of a shed).
Every Touched standing in the Barn's yard wears the Barn down; let it go to
nothing and they have it. Between waves the rangers patch it up and a crate
comes down behind it.

When the last wave falls, the ground shakes. Dust goes up off Boulder Bluff,
something roars, and the rangers turn to look ("What... was THAT?" "That came
from Boulder Bluff."). Then the tally on the door is rubbed out and chalked
back to **0**. Brutus is heard, not seen. Holding the Barn earns **Days Since
Brutus: 0** and the **Touched coat** (the Touched's own glowing veins, to
wear); holding it with all seven rangers standing, **Every Ranger Home**. The
Gazette prints a special edition: THE BARN HOLDS.

### Act I: *The Carrot Patch* (playable)

The tally says nought, and the Barn Rangers want to know what the Touched
came out of the Patch for. Something there is glowing. Opens once the Barn's
been held.

1. **The glowing carrots.** Five of them, on a trail from the Barn out into
   the Carrot Patch, the Acorn's gold-green shining out of them in veins. Only
   one glows bright at a time, its light going up over the grass; pull it up
   and the next lights. The Touched come up out of the dirt round each one.
   They're warm, and they hum.
2. **Burn the Patch.** Three rings in the Patch, its two rows and the bed
   beside them. Stand in a ring, with none of the Touched in it, until it goes
   up, while they rush you from the Boulder Bluff side. The rangers hold the
   rings with you. When all three are alight the whole Patch burns, and the
   ground starts to drum.
3. **Wake him at Boulder Bluff.** Get close and Brutus comes down off the
   Bluff for you. The Warren answers: eight rogues who saw the smoke come
   gliding down to fight him with you (Cottontail Kate, Hops McGee, Acorn Al,
   Ember, Rocco, Bog Boss, Timber and Cheeks McGee).

When he falls, a gold-green light swells in his chest and he shrinks, smaller
and smaller in his own dust, until what's lying there is a little grey bunny:
**Little Bru**, the runt of the Carrot Patch warren, who ate the glowing
carrots and the Kernel they'd grown round. Over him hangs what was inside
him: **the first Starfall Kernel**, gold-green, humming, the first anyone in
the Warren has seen. "It's... it's just a bunny." "Little Bru? Is that you?"
He looks up at it, the way he used to look at the stars. "Look at it glow.
That's a Kernel. The FIRST Kernel." "It's humming. Like it's calling
something. Across the lake." (To be continued in Act II: *The Clockwork
Decree*.)

Beating him earns **The First Kernel** and the **Patch fire** beat-down burst
(embers and a puff of smoke); doing it with every ranger standing, **Nobody
Singed**. The Gazette's special edition: BRUTUS IS LITTLE BRU AGAIN.

### Act II: *The Clockwork Decree* (playable)

The first Kernel won't stop humming, and it hums at the House on the shore,
so the Barn Rangers take it across the lake (Buck Toothington carries it),
in at the window and down into the children's toy room. The toy box stands
open: they didn't lock it. Critters are marching stiffly round the room with
keys turning in their backs, the **Wound**, and a red light sweeps across
the floor from high on the bookshelf, where two red eyes are watching. Opens
once the Carrot Patch is done.

1. **Free the Wound.** Four of the Warren's rogues went into the House and
   never came back: Turbo, Trash Panda, Hopscotch and Twitch, Wound now, each
   holding a landmark (the Loop-de-Loop, Brick City, Dollhouse Manor, Domino
   Run) with a few more of the Wound round them. Beat one down and it
   doesn't go down: it winds down, its key slowing. Pull the key out of its
   back before it winds itself back up, and it's free, and fights with you.
2. **Break his winding stations.** Three tin wind-up machines as big as a
   shed, painted like old toys (in Crayon Canyon, by the Toy Chest and at the
   Pillow Fort): where the Wound are wound. Each winds another out of its
   chute every so often while you're near. Tin takes the sting out of
   carrots, but not on the drum, where the spring is, and a blast does them
   in. Broken, they come apart: the drum rolls off, the gears fly, the key
   goes up and comes down somewhere.
3. **Storm Block Castle.** His. The Wound hold the courtyard; take it with
   none of them in the ring and the Warren's flag goes up over his castle.
   Then the eyes on the bookshelf find you, and he jumps: the Tin Tyrant,
   across the whole room, down in front of his castle.

When he falls, the key in his back slows and stops. A gold-green light comes
out of the hole it turns in, and he shrinks in his own steam until what's
lying there is a tin toy robot no taller than a bunny: **Model No. 7**, from
the toy room windowsill. Out of his back comes what was in his winding drum:
**the second Starfall Kernel**. "He's... a toy. He's just a toy." "Model
Number Seven. It says so on his foot." He gets up, turns to them, and does
what he did for thirty years on the windowsill: three steps, a beep and
sparks. Buck brings out the first Kernel, and the two pull at each other,
and then both the same way: "Toward the hamster cage." (To be continued in
Act III: *Foam at the Falls*.)

Beating him earns **The Second Kernel** and the **Clockwork** beat-down
burst (cogs and sparks); doing it with every ranger standing, **Nobody Left
Wound**. The Gazette's special edition: THE TIN TYRANT IS A TOY AGAIN.

### Act III: *Foam at the Falls* (playable)

Both Kernels pull at the hamster cage, so the Barn Rangers follow them in,
over the glass and down into the bedding. Under it Nibbles has dug his
tunnels from here toward the toy room, toward the hum, and his water bottle
has never once run dry: the third Kernel, the one no bigger than a seed, is
in it. Opens once the Clockwork Decree is done.

1. **Cave in his burrows.** His burrows are dug into the hamster's heaps of
   bedding, and the Touched keep coming up out of them while you're near.
   Nothing you can shoot shuts one: a blast in its mouth caves it in (a
   grenade: a crate of them comes down; a rocket; the rangers' rockets too).
2. **Dam Water Bottle Falls.** Carry chew blocks, one at a time, over your
   head, slowly, into the ring under the bottle's spout while the Touched come
   at you; knocked off your feet, you drop it. Three, and the falls are
   dammed: the last drips stop and the puddle drains away.
3. **Something wants a drink.** The bedding by the dam heaves, and Nibbles
   bursts up out of it.

When he falls, a gold-green light swells in his cheeks and he shrinks, in a
froth of foam, until what's sitting there is a little golden hamster with
very full cheeks: the children's Nibbles, a good boy. He squeaks and spits
out **the third Starfall Kernel**. "He's... tiny." "Nibbles? Good boy. Who's
a good boy?" Buck Toothington brings out the other two, and the three find
each other in the air and turn round each other, brighter and brighter.
Then the floor shakes, and the Briar comes up through the bedding all round
them: "All three of them. Together." "The Briar! It's coming up through the
floor!" (To be continued in the Finale: *The Starfall*.)

Beating him earns **The Third Kernel** and the **Sunflower seeds** beat-down
burst; doing it with every ranger standing, **Nobody Foamed**. The Gazette's
special edition: NIBBLES IS A GOOD BOY AGAIN.

### The Finale: *The Starfall* (playable)

The Briar's vines closed over the three Kernels and pulled them down through
the bedding, and its roots carried them under the lake, home to where the
Acorn fell. By nightfall Boulder Bluff was glowing gold-green over the whole
island. The Barn Rangers start at the Big Red Barn, by night, the stars out.
Opens once Foam at the Falls is done.

1. **Cut the roots.** Three of the Briar's taproots have broken up out of the
   island round the Barn's side of the Bluff, the Kernels' light running
   along them in pulses toward it. Break the knot of each, where it came up:
   carrots barely scratch the bark, a blast does it real harm (you start with
   grenades, and a crate brings more and a rocket launcher). The Touched grow
   up out of the ground round a knot while you're near. Cut, a root withers
   grey and sinks back into the ground. "Cut the roots, and we starve it."
2. **Get inside the Briar.** With the roots cut it calls up the Briar: its
   wall comes up round the Bluff and closes in (outside it, its thorns
   bite), the Touched pour down off the Bluff at you, and the Warren
   answers: the rogues who came for Brutus and the ones you unwound in the
   toy room glide down to fight beside you. "We saw the light from across the
   lake! We're with you!"
3. **Stop its heart.** The ring shut, the Bluff heaves, and the **Briar
   Heart** comes up out of it in the husk of the Acorn. Break its pods while
   they're open and the Kernels fly free; split open, it closes the Briar in
   tighter, and its heart's out.

When it falls the light goes out of the Touched, all of them at once, and
they blink and sit down, critters again. The three Kernels come down
together over the husk; its pieces lift off the Bluff and close round them,
and there it is, whole for the first time since it fell: **the Starfall
Acorn**, gold and humming, as big as the Big Red Barn. "They're putting
themselves back together. It's the ACORN." It rises off the Bluff, slowly
and then faster, up into the stars, while the Briar's wall withers away; at
the front of the crowd watching it go are the three it made into giants,
small again: Little Bru, Model No. 7 and Nibbles. "It's going home." It's a
point of light, then a star, a new one over Boulder Bluff. "Look. Up there.
It's a star again." Little Bru is counting: "...a hundred and twelve. A
hundred and thirteen." The Kernel Hunt is complete; the Acorn's gone home,
and the Briar's just a briar. (It still closes in wherever critters fight.
Old Mother Clover says it isn't looking for anything any more.)

Beating it earns **The Starfall** and the **Shooting stars** beat-down
burst; doing it with every ranger standing, **Nobody Pricked**. The Gazette's
special edition: THE ACORN GOES HOME.

What's already built that it would stand on:

- **The Trail** (Run): a chain of stops, each with a goal, helmets as lives
  and boons carrying over. A quest act is a Trail with its stops written ahead
  of time instead of picked from a map.
- **Team Battles**: two sides with friendly fire off, blue tags, a squad that
  sticks by you, two balloons. The Touched are the other side.
- **Boss Battles**: the bosses, their arrival cinematics (BossIntro), the
  rogues' shouts and the records that open their stories.
- **The cast**: 49 rogues in their own voices to be your squad, with their
  grudges and gangs.
- **Rewind** and the kill cam, the Warren Gazette (to report the quest's news),
  Milestones for each act.
