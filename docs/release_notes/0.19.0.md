## Bunny Beat Down v0.19.0: Crackjaw, the Nutcracker General

**The Toy Box has a second boss.** Pick him in the main menu's Boss row with Toy Box Bash and a Boss Battle: Crackjaw, a wooden nutcracker as tall as the bookcase, his painted eyes lit ice blue. He was carved to fight the Mouse King, the night the Starfall Acorn came in through the window he bit a chip off it, and now every critter in the toy room is the Mouse King's army. A bunny in an acorn helmet is a nut.

- **Quick March.** A drum roll and a red lane on the floor, then down it he comes, every stamp of his boots knocking critters flying and kicking apart any toy fort in his way. Get out of the lane.
- **Sabre Sweep.** His sabre swung flat across the floor in front of him (and back again, once he's angry). Jump it as it comes.
- **Crack!** Up close he bows and snaps his jaw shut on whatever's in front of him. If he misses, his jaw hangs open for a moment: the best place there is to shoot him.
- **Walnut Volley.** Giant walnuts lobbed high, each cracking open on its own red ring.
- **TIMBER!** Shoot his legs (rockets and grenades count three times over) and he starts to wobble. Enough, and he goes over like a felled pine along a red strip, flattens whoever's under him, and lies there with his head on the floor, taking double.

His wooden arms, legs, jaw and hat swing rigid on their pegs like a real nutcracker's. The model is the Cracxus festivus by Brian Trepanier (CC BY 4.0), who also made Brutus's. Every rogue has three new lines in their own voice: "It's Crackjaw! Mind your acorns!", "Shoot his legs! He'll go right over!" and "TIMBER! Get his head!". He has his own four chapters on the Trophy Room's Bosses page and a Gazette special edition when he falls, and six sounds made in code: his wooden footsteps, his jaw's clack, a walnut cracking, his sabre's swish, a toy bugle and a drum roll.

**Beat him for the Nutshells** beat-down burst (walnut shell flying, and a glint of his gold braid), and **The Big Five**: beat all five bosses for 500 acorns (The Big Four is now any four of them).
